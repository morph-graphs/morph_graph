How well does Gemini 3.8 Flash answer EPIC-Morph?

Gemini 3.8 Flash on the first version of the EPIC-Morph question bank: 449 multiple-choice questions about 12 egocentric kitchen videos of one participant (HD-EPIC P09). It was run once with the video and once with no video, as a control. This report covers the questions as they stood before this week's fixes (epic-morph 04fb5d5).

model gemini-3.8-flash · thinking HIGH · media HIGH · 1 fps code morph_graph a504811 questions epic-morph 04fb5d5 runs 7625435 (video) · 7625437 (text-only)
  • With the video, Gemini gets 75% of the non-yes/no questions right; without it, 42%. The video adds about 33 points, and it helps in every one of the 12 videos.
  • Its clear weakness is counting. On "how many X did I use?" it scores 50%, undercounting far more often than overcounting, and it gets worse as the true count grows.
  • Likely weaknesses: tracking what is in a particular container at the moment the question is asked, and how long something has been on the hob. The errors lean the same way every time, but the samples are small.
  • Several question types could be answered without the video, because of how their options were written. Event-order questions were 100% correct with no video at all. We have since fixed the options; see Problems in the question bank.
Setup

What the model saw

Questions

449 questions of 22 templated types (form), each shown as 4 options. 222 are streaming questions: the model sees the video only up to the moment the question is asked (t_ask), cut with ffmpeg and checked so that no frame comes from after t_ask. The other 227 see the whole recording. 129 questions have a yes/no answer.

Two runs

Video: each video and clip is uploaded once, and its tokens are cached for all the questions that share it. The answer comes back as one letter. Text-only control: the same question and options, with no video and no narration, and the prompt says the video is unavailable. It measures how much can be answered from the question and options alone.

What does not predict difficulty

Reasoning hops and video length make little difference

On non-yes/no questions, 3-hop questions are no harder than 1-hop ones, and the longest recordings score best. The bank's own guessable flag is the only field here that separates hard questions from easy ones.

Failure modes

Where Gemini goes wrong

1 · Counting distinct objects: 50%

Accuracy by the true count, across all 38 count questions. It undercounts in 15 cases and overcounts in 4. Bowls account for 8 of its 19 misses. These questions also draw the most thinking, about 29k tokens each.

3 · How long has it been on the hob: 69%

7 of its 8 wrong answers are overestimates, some by 30+ minutes ("about 4 minutes" answered as "about 41 minutes"). It seems to time the vessel from an earlier session on the hob, not the current one.

n = 26, all streaming. The interval (50–83%) overlaps the average, so this is likely rather than established.

2 · What is in a container at the moment the question is asked: 66–70%

Four question types test this: checklist (67.9%, n=28), streaming_last_added (65.5%, n=29), first_use_after_retrieval (66.7%, n=12) and what_else_in (70.0%, n=10). Their errors lean the same way. All 9 checklist errors include an ingredient that had not been added yet: Gemini seems to fill in the recipe instead of stopping at the question time. On the other types it mixes up similar containers, or answers from what a container is normally used for.

Caveat: some of these "errors" may be mistakes in the bank's answers. In the two checklist cases we checked frame by frame, the video supports Gemini. Salt goes into the smaller saucepan at 13:10–13:13 in P09-20240624-160737, and something red is spooned into the white bowl at 02:16–02:19 in P09-20240623-120359, but the bank's answers include neither. The answers come from narrations, which miss small actions like these, so the 26 container-state failures should be checked against the video before this area is scaled up.

Composite (2-hop) questions, by the question they wrap

Describing the object indirectly ("the cup I used for the hibiscus drink") costs nothing on put_away_at_end: 86.7% against 87.7% when asked directly. The other composite groups are too small to judge.

Question bank

Problems in the question bank: answerable without the video

The text-only run showed that four question types could be answered from how their options were written. Each problem below is measured with a simple rule that never looks at the video. The fixes are on the epic-morph branch mcq-option-cues (a240b88).

How far to trust these numbers

Caveats

  • One run, one participant, 12 videos. Nothing measures run-to-run variation, and questions cluster by video and type. The gap between video and text-only holds video by video; the per-type differences mostly do not reach significance.
  • The text-only run is not a pure ablation. Its prompt says the video is unavailable, and it gets no narration either. A control that gives the model the narration instead of the video would separate what it sees from what it reasons.
  • The same model family wrote the wrong options. Gemini 3.8 Flash wrote them from the narrations; that could affect scores in either direction.
  • Some of the bank's answers are probably wrong (see the container caveat above).
  • Independent check. Gemini was asked to challenge these claims. It rated the headline gap "partially supported" and the list of weaknesses "not supported" on significance grounds; counting is the one weakness that clears it.
Cost and time

About $59 and 47 minutes for both runs

Published paid-tier prices for gemini-3.8-flash until 31 Dec 2026: input $0.75, output including thinking $3.75, cached input $0.075 per million tokens, plus $0.50 per million tokens per hour for cache storage. Prices double from 1 Jan 2027. The heaviest single answer used 62,915 of the 65,536-token output cap on thinking, so no answer was cut off.

Next

Next steps

  • Merge the bank fixes (mcq-option-cues) and re-run Gemini on the 168 changed questions (about $10–15).
  • Decide on put_away_at_end: generate more "put away" questions, cap "left out" at half the type, or report it against its majority baseline.
  • Also ask checklist questions before the first addition and after the last, so that "none" and "all" can be correct answers.
  • Scale the dataset toward the weak areas: counting with 3 or more of the same object; container state where the recipe order misleads; duration questions with several on/off sessions. First check the container-state answers against the video.
  • Other models: a Qwen backend is under way on the same pipeline, with one backend file per model.
